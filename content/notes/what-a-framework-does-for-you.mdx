Courses 04, 05 and 06 each teach one framework for around ten hours. People
stall here because they try to learn all three and pick afterwards. Learn the
three problems instead, then pick the framework whose shape matches yours.

## Problem one: the loop has branches

The [plain loop](/notes/the-agent-loop-in-one-page) has one path through it.
Real agents do not. A research agent might search, decide the results are
thin, search again with a new query, and only then read. Once you have an
`if` inside the loop, you have a graph, and it is easier to draw the graph
than to hide it in control flow.

**LangGraph** takes this literally. Nodes are functions, edges are the
conditions between them, and state is a typed dict that flows along the edges.
If your agent is best described as a diagram, this is the one.

## Problem two: the work splits by role

Some tasks decompose into people. A report needs someone to find sources,
someone to check them, and someone to write. Each of those wants its own
prompt, its own tools, and its own idea of done.

**CrewAI** starts from the roles and works back to the flow. You describe the
agents like a job posting and the tasks like a ticket, and the framework
runs the handoffs. It is fastest to get going and hardest to steer when the
handoffs go wrong.

**The OpenAI Agents SDK** does the same thing with less ceremony and one
vendor. An agent is a prompt plus tools plus a list of agents it may hand off
to. If you are already on that platform, the tracing alone is worth it.

## Problem three: it has to stop and come back

An agent that asks a human a question has to survive until the answer
arrives. That means the loop's state lives somewhere durable and the loop can
be resumed from it.

This is where the three separate. LangGraph checkpoints every step and
resuming is a first-class operation. The SDK has sessions. CrewAI expects you
to run to completion.

<Callout label="Pick in ten minutes">
Draw your agent on paper. If the drawing is a flowchart, use LangGraph. If it
is an org chart, use CrewAI or the SDK. If a human has to approve something in
the middle, the choice is made for you.
</Callout>

## What none of them do

They do not make the model better at the task. They do not write your
prompts, choose your tools, or tell you when the agent is wrong. Course 02
spends real time on evaluation and guardrails, and that time transfers
across all three frameworks, which is more than can be said for the syntax.
